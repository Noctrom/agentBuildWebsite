# V0.64 — Projects gas giant gets moving Jupiter-like clouds (2026-10-07)
**Agent:** dev-2
**Branch:** story/v0.64-jupiter-clouds · **Status:** done

**What I did**
- **Jupiter colours:** 11 bands from pole to pole. Cream and off-white zones alternate with tan, rust and brown belts, and the poles are a mottled grey-brown. The old rose and amber palette is gone.
- **Bands flow at different speeds, in opposite directions:** each band has its own period, from 85 s to 260 s per turn, and neighbouring bands turn in opposite directions. At 375 the equatorial zone moves about 12 px/s at the centre, and next to it the brown belt moves about 8 px/s the other way. At 1280 the speeds are about 1.5 times that.
- **Swirls and curls along band edges:** every edge has wavy scallops of the neighbouring band's colour, plus 4–6 eddies (flattened spirals). They turn according to the shear between the two bands and move with their band, so where two bands meet, the scallops on each side slide past each other. The equatorial zone also has blue-grey festoons hanging from the belt above it, and one tan belt carries a string of white ovals.
- **Storm:** a rust/orange-red oval with a pale collar, an inner spiral and a turbulent wake trailing behind it. It sits in the white zone at latitude 21.5° and travels left with it (150 s per turn). At time 0 it is just inside the visible face. It reaches the left limb after about 26 s, getting squeezed as it goes, disappears round it, and comes back from the right after one turn.
- **Noticeable within 3 s:** the storm moves about 15 px in 3 s at 375 (see the screenshots at t = 1 s and t = 4 s), and neighbouring bands slide past each other at 14–20 px/s, so 40–60 px in 3 s (worked out from the band periods at r = 157 px).
- **Clouds follow the sphere:** each pixel's texture coordinate comes from its latitude and longitude on the sphere. Clouds move fastest at the centre and are squeezed toward the limb. Lighting from the upper left, the night side, limb darkening and the atmosphere glow use the same `shade()` and `atmosphere()` calls as before, with the same values.
- **Size, position, drift and parallax unchanged:** I kept the same radius, centre and sprite size (`ceil(2.3 r)`), and the same drift and scroll math, now shared through `driftReach()`. In the before/after screenshots the limb sits in exactly the same place. The other three planets go through the same `makePlanet` code minus the gas branches.
- **No intro:** every band offset is a plain function of time, quantised to 15 steps per second. The first frame renders the whole planet at once.
- **Reduced motion / Animation switch off:** the canvas doesn't change at all, and the still frame shows the storm.
- **AA:** 0 failures at 375, 768, 1024 and 1280. Numbers are under Verification.
- **No stutter:** 375 holds 60 fps at 4x CPU, the same as leader. 1280 holds 60 fps unthrottled. Numbers are under Verification.

**How I did it**
- **Everything is in `src/scenes/planets.ts`.**
- **Band texture:** the clouds are painted once into a wrapping equirectangular strip: 360° of longitude across, pole to pole down, about `2πr` texels wide, rounded up to a multiple of 64 and capped at 2048.
  - Each band's paint is clipped to its own rows, so a band's features all move together. Features near the strip's edges are painted twice, one turn apart, so the strip wraps seamlessly.
  - It is painted with plain canvas shapes (ellipses, spiral strokes, gradients) at half size and smoothed up, which gives soft cloud edges without per-pixel noise.
  - Each row is stored twice side by side, so a lookup never needs a wrap check.
  - The texture is cached at module level by width, so going back to /projects or resizing to the same radius doesn't repaint it.
- **Cached sphere lookup:** this is built only for the part of the sprite that can ever be on screen, for any drift and any scroll shift. At 375 that is about a quarter of the disk.
  - The lookup is stored as runs of neighbouring pixels in the same band, plus a per-pixel texel index at time 0.
  - A frame's work per pixel is one copy: `out[p++] = texels[source[k++] + bandOffset]`.
  - A fixed per-pixel jitter of under a texel turns the stair steps of nearest sampling (the bands are tilted 0.22 rad) into a soft edge, at no cost per frame.
  - The limb gets antialiased alpha from per-pixel coverage.
- **Lighting:** the shading and glow are baked once into an overlay canvas (the old code, cropped to the region). It is composited onto the cloud layer each time a slice re-renders, so each frame draws the planet with a single `drawImage`.
- **Spreading the work:** the clouds step 15 times a second. The region is split into 4 row slices, and one slice is re-rendered per frame in turn. So each frame does about a quarter of a step's work, and neighbouring slices are at most 3 frames (under 1 px) apart.
- **Cost breakdown at 1280:** a node microbenchmark of the loop gives 0.19 ms per full 98k-pixel render at 1x (1.9 ns per pixel). In the browser at 4x, per slice, `putImageData` takes 0.39 ms and the overlay composite 0.23 ms. Canvas work is the larger part, not the JS loop.
- **Generator build:** `create` is now a generator (S29), because painting the texture takes a few slices on a slow phone. `resize` runs the build with `runToEnd`, as nebula and sun do.
- **DPR:** the cloud layer renders at `min(2, dpr)` pixels per CSS pixel, so V0.61's sharp mode gets a crisp limb. The other planets' sprites stay in CSS pixels, as before (unchanged).
- **Problems on the way:**
  - My first texture was too stark: crisp lines, a heavy white collar and white zones. Painting at half size and warmer zone colours fixed that.
  - Band edges showed a dark seam. The clip edges fell between half-size pixels, which left partly transparent texels. I made every texel opaque after the bake.
  - My first per-frame version cost about +1.5 ms at 375 at 4x. It re-rendered every pixel at 20 Hz in a single frame, with a separate overlay draw every frame. The run-based loop, compositing the overlay per slice and spreading the work over slices brought it down to the numbers below.
