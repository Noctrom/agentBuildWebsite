# V0.44 — Behind the Scenes black hole comes alive (2026-10-05)
**Agent:** dev-2
**Branch:** story/v0.44-black-hole-alive · **Status:** done

**What I did**
- **Moving within 3 seconds:** in 3 s the inner band of the disk turns about 107°, and the hot spots travel 106–129° each. At 375 the inner edge moves about 28 px/s, and at 1280 about 60 px/s. On Behind the Scenes, 85–90k colour channels change around the hole between 0.5 s and 1.5 s after arriving, on both a direct load and a client-side navigation.
- **Swirling disk, inner edge faster:** the disk is split at 2.2 shadow radii. The streaks of the hot inner band turn at 0.62 rad/s (one turn in about 10 s). The outer disk turns at 0.05 rad/s (about 2 min a turn, close to the old 0.045). That makes the inner edge about 12 times faster than the outer edge.
- **Hot spots:** three bright clumps, each with a short trail, orbit the inner edge (1.62–1.84 r) at 0.62–0.75 rad/s. On the approaching (left) side they are at full brightness, and on the receding side at 25%. They pass in front of the shadow on the near side and behind it on the far side.
- **Photon ring flicker:** the ring is now stroked live. Its opacity is 0.8 × (1 + 0.25 × f(t)), so it stays between 0.6 and 1.0. f(t) is a sum of three sines at 0.3, 0.7 and 1.1 Hz.
- **Lensing shimmer:** three parts. The lensed arc over the top shimmers in brightness: the old slow pulse plus a 0.7 Hz wave, between 0.8 and 1.0. While a hot spot is behind the hole, its bent image runs along the arc as a glint. And 6 of the 30 stretched starlight arcs twinkle on their own waves (periods 1.4–3.5 s).
- **No intro:** every effect is a plain function of scene time. Frame 0 looks like any other frame, and nothing replays on load or navigation.
- **AA on animated frames:** 0 failures at 375, 768, 1024 and 1280. Numbers are under Verification.
- **Reduced motion:** with the OS setting or the footer Animation switch off, the canvas does not change at all. The still frame at time 0 shows the whole hole: one hot spot on the left, one in front of the shadow, a glint over the top and the ring.
- **No visible stutter:** 375 holds 60 fps (242 frames in 4 s) at 4× CPU throttle, just as leader does. Without throttling, both widths run at 60 fps. Cost numbers are under Verification.
- **375 and desktop:** checked at 375×812, 768×1024, 1024×768, 1280×800 and 1920×1080.

**How I did it**
- Everything is in `src/scenes/blackHole.ts`. The layout (`layoutFor`) is unchanged from V0.43.
- **The cost problem:** the canvas is software-rendered here. A rotated, squashed draw of the full disk texture costs about 1 ms at 1x CPU at 1280, which is about 4 ms at 4× throttle. My first try rotated five cross-faded rings every frame. It added +5 ms at 375 and +11 ms at 1280. I dropped it.
- **Final design, two layers:**
  - **Outer layer:** the gradient, the outer streaks and the dark lanes. It is re-rendered 12 times a second, exactly as before V0.44, so its cost is unchanged.
  - **Inner band:** streaks only, drawn additively on top. Its streak layout repeats every quarter turn, so the band has only 152 distinct frames (60 per second over the 2.5 s a quarter turn takes). Each frame is rendered the first time it is shown and then reused, so after a few seconds the fast band costs only a copy.
  - Squashed to a thin, moving band, the 4-fold repeat doesn't show. Each band streak is shorter than a quarter turn, so it never meets its own copy.
  - The cache is about 1.8 MB at 375 and 7.8 MB at 1280×800. It is capped at 12 MB (`INNER_CACHE_BYTES`). Above that, roughly when the visible area is about 1000 px or more on its shorter side, the band is rendered live each frame. I checked that at 1920×1080.
- **Draw count:** each small `drawImage` costs about 10–15 µs at 4×, so I kept the count low: 3 spots with 2-dot trails, glints only for the spot heads, and 6 twinkling arcs (the other 24 stay baked in the backdrop sprite). An earlier version that cross-faded three full-size streak sprites over the lensed arc was too expensive, so the arc shimmer now comes from a brightness wave plus the glints.
- **Same average brightness:** following V0.40, every twinkle swings around the old value. The arcs use alpha × (1 + d·sin), which averages to the old alpha. The lens averages 0.9, as before. The ring averages 0.8, against about 0.81 before (0.9 inside a 0.9–1.0 sprite). Only the hot spots and glints add light.
- **How I guarantee WCAG 2.3.1 for the ring:**
  1. **By construction:** the three ring waves are the 3rd, 7th and 11th harmonics of 0.1 Hz. So the flicker is a trigonometric polynomial of degree 11 with a 10 s period. Its slope can change sign at most 22 times per period, which means at most 1.1 brightness peaks per second.
  2. **Checked numerically:** sampled at 1 ms over 60 s, there were 66 peaks, and never more than 2 in any 1 s window. The lens shimmer had at most 1 peak per second.
  3. **Measured amplitude:** the ring's on-screen relative luminance swung only 0.0374–0.0414 (Δ 0.004) at 1280 over 10 s. WCAG counts a flash only at a change of 0.1 or more, over 25% of a 10° field. The ring is a 1–2 px line, and the canvas is shown at 0.2 opacity.
  - Every other wave is at most 0.71 Hz.
- I kept the starlight arcs, lensed streaks and outer-disk streaks on their old seeds, so the scene still looks like V0.43. Only the inner band's streaks come from a new seed (2605).

**Follow-ups**
- **Perf at 375:** +0.68 ms at 4× is over the +0.5 ms aim, though 375 still holds 60 fps. The extra cost is spread out: the band's two draws (~0.25 ms), spots and glints (~0.1 ms), twinkling arcs (~0.1 ms), and the ring stroke and lens wave. To get more back, the options are fewer twinkling arcs, or no trails on the spots. V0.48's note asked for a recheck after V0.44/V0.45: Behind the Scenes at 375 is now about 87% busy at 4×, against 83.5% on leader.
- **Notes for V0.45 (orbiting planet):**
  - Draw order in `draw()`: backdrop and arcs → far half of disk and band → far spots → lensed arc → glints → ring → shadow → near half → near spots. A planet on the far side of its orbit goes before the shadow (it is hidden behind it). On the near side it goes after the near half of the disk.
  - Disk plane: a point at angle a and radius R (in shadow radii) is at x = R·r·cos a, y = R·r·sin a·0.15, rotated by `PLANE_ANGLE` around (cx, cy). `drawSpots` already does this math. Angles decrease over time, so the near side (sin > 0) moves right. A planet orbiting with the disk should use the same sense. The far side is sin < 0.
  - Keep the planet inside the area width. The disk already fills up to 96% of it at 375.
  - Budget: one planet sprite plus a stream is a few small draws. Bake the bands into a sprite once, and don't re-render any big layer per frame (see the cost notes above). At 375 the page is at about 87% busy at 4×.
  - Reduced motion shows time 0. Choose the planet's starting angle so it is fully visible at t = 0.
