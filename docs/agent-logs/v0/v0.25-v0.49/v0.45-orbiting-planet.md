# V0.45 — Blue gas giant orbits the black hole (2026-10-06)
**Agent:** dev-2
**Branch:** story/v0.45-orbiting-planet · **Status:** done

**What I did**
- **Blue gas giant, a quarter of the shadow's width:** the planet's radius is 0.25 shadow radii, so it is half a shadow radius across: 15 px at 375 and 32 px at 1280. It has horizontal bands in five shades of blue, from deep navy to pale ice blue, with a slight wave along each band and darker poles and limb.
- **Orbits in the disk plane, with the same tilt:** it moves on a circle of 3.4 shadow radii inside the disk, using the same plane math as the hot spots (squash 0.15, tilt `PLANE_ANGLE`). Its bands lie parallel to the disk and bow slightly, because its axis is the disk's axis and we see the disk a little from above. It turns the same way as the disk, so the near side moves right.
- **In front on the near side, hidden on the far side:** on the near side it is drawn after the near half of the disk, so it passes in front of the disk and the shadow. On the far side it is drawn before the lensed arc and the shadow, so it goes behind them: the shadow hides it completely as it passes behind the hole (checked in a crop at 1280, t ≈ 19 s).
- **One orbit in 30 s:** from the start position it moves about 57 px in the first 3 s at 375, and about 120 px at 1280. A visitor reading for a minute sees two full orbits. It is also lit from the hole: the side facing the hole is brighter, so you see its lit face beside and behind the hole and its darker night side in front.
- **Faint blue gas stream:** a tapering blue ribbon leaves the planet's inner side, falls inward and curves round into the inner disk (from 3.4 to 2.0 shadow radii, over 1.4 rad). It keeps that shape relative to the planet, so it moves with it. Like the planet, it is split into far and near parts by the same rule, so the shadow hides the parts behind the hole. It never covers the planet: it starts at the planet's centre, and any stretch that touches the planet's disk on screen is drawn just before the planet, which is opaque.
- **AA on animated frames:** 0 failures at 375, 768, 1024 and 1280, the same lowest numbers as leader. Numbers are under Verification.
- **Reduced motion:** the planet starts at angle 2.3: on the near side, front left of the hole, fully visible, with its stream running in front of the shadow's lower edge. With the OS setting or the footer switch, the canvas does not change, and the still frame shows the planet and its stream.
- **No visible stutter:** 375 holds 60 fps at 4× CPU throttle (241 frames in 4 s, the same as leader). Without throttling, both widths run at 60 fps.
- **375 and desktop:** checked at 375×812, 768×1024, 1024×768, 1280×800 and 1920×1080. The orbit reaches 3.65 shadow radii, inside the disk's 4.8, so the planet stays inside the area width at every size.

**How I did it**
- Everything is in `src/scenes/blackHole.ts`: two new classes, `Planet` and `PlanetStream`, plus a few lines in `draw()`. The layout and the V0.44 layers are unchanged.
- **Planet sprite:** the bands, limb and coverage are worked out once per size, in one pixel pass with the trigonometry (≤ 1,200 pixels). The lighting changes with the orbit, so there are 36 lighting steps (10° each). Each step's sprite is made the first time it is shown, by scaling the base pixels by the light. That is cheap, so after the first orbit the planet costs only one small `drawImage`.
  - My first version baked each lighting step from scratch, with 72 steps. An in-page A/B test showed those bakes cost about 0.13 ms a frame on average during the first orbit at 375 at 4× (about 1.7 ms each). After the change it is down to almost nothing.
- **Stream:** 16 points along the curve, recomputed each frame (a few trig calls), then filled as a tapering ribbon: a wide, faint sheath and a narrow, brighter core that covers only the first 60% of the stream. Together they fade it out toward the disk.
  - My first version stroked a line with a per-frame gradient. It looked like a stiff white needle, because the curve is nearly flat seen edge-on. The tapered ribbon reads as gas. A gradient fill cost about 0.1 ms a frame at 375 at 4×, so I used two flat fills instead.
- **Draw order:** far stream parts and the far planet go after the far spots, before the lensed arc and the shadow. Near stream parts and the near planet go after the near spots. Each stretch of the stream is assigned by the sign of sin(angle) at its ends, just like the hot spots.
- **Stream direction (a judgement call):** the story says the stream "trails off the planet". Gas pulled inward orbits faster than the planet, so a real stream from a planet toward a hole runs ahead of it, curving forward and inward. I drew it that way, since it shows the gas being pulled in. Flipping it to lag behind is a one-character change (the sign before `STREAM_SWEEP`).
- **Brightness:** the planet is opaque and at full brightness, like everything else the engine shows at 0.2 opacity. The stream uses alpha 0.3 and 0.45.
