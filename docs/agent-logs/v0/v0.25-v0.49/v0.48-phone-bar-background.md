# V0.48 — Phone top bar shows the space background (2026-10-05)
**Agent:** dev-1

**What I did**
- Below lg, the top bar shows the same space background as the page behind it. The frosted glass and the line under it are gone. With animation off, the bar's pixels differ from the bare page background by at most 1 colour level per channel, so there is no seam, tint or line. With animation on, the change across the bar's bottom edge is under 1.3 levels on average.
- Page text, buttons, cards and images scrolling under the bar are not visible through it. With content hidden, the bar's pixels are byte-identical to the normal view.
- The open hamburger menu follows the same rule. The backdrop grows to cover the bar plus the open menu, so the background shows and page content under the menu does not. The menu's border line and glass are gone too.
- Contrast passes AA over the brightest frame at 375 and 768, without the glass. Hamburger icon: fg 10.43. Menu labels: fg 9.85, muted 5.34, accent 5.09.
- Works at 375 and 768. Desktop (lg and up) is pixel-identical to before at 1024 and 1280 on all 7 routes.
- V0.35 and V0.42 behaviours are unchanged: skip link, focus and anchors landing below the bar, scroll-to-top on navigation, menu closing on navigation, and the auto-pause toast.

**How I did it**
- I chose option (b), painting a copy of the background in the bar, through a new `backgroundMirror.ts` module. I rejected (a), clipping the content layer, because the clip must move with the page on every scroll frame. That runs on the main thread, while scrolling runs on the compositor (and on iOS momentum), so the clip lags. For a frame, content shows under the bar, or the content just below it is cut off. Every CSS way to fix a mask to the viewport (`mask-attachment`, `element()`, scroll-driven clip-path) is either unsupported in Safari or Firefox, or not composited. The copy has no scroll dependency at all, because the bar and the background canvas are both fixed to the viewport. Making `main` a scroll container was ruled out, as the notes say.
- The copy is made by the engine itself (`BackgroundEngine.paintCopy`), right after it draws each frame (`drawAll` calls `repaintMirrors()`). So it is never a frame behind, and it follows every fade, crossfade, scene change, resize, still frame (animation off) and the `scrollY` parallax. Each layer is drawn with `globalAlpha` = the opacity its canvas is shown at (`opacity × BACKGROUND_MAX_OPACITY`). It uses the same backing scale (dpr) and the source rect for the bar's spot.
- The mirror canvas is opaque (`alpha: false`). It is filled with `--theme-bg`, and then the layers are drawn on top. That composites exactly like the page, which is the canvas over the solid body colour. Because it is opaque, it hides content and the compositor can skip what it covers.
- The canvas's rect is measured by a ResizeObserver, not every frame. The sticky bar always sits at the viewport's top-left, and the menu opening changes its size. With animation off, nothing redraws per frame. The mirror repaints from the layer canvases' last frame when it resizes, for example when the menu opens.
- `Header.tsx`: a `Backdrop` layer replaces the glass div. It is absolutely positioned at the top of the header, `-z-10`, `lg:hidden`, and its height is set to the bar plus the open menu, measured by a ResizeObserver on both (the closed menu is display:none, so it adds 0). It is one layer behind both bar and menu rather than a background on `<nav>`, because the nav scrolls on its own and a child canvas would scroll with it.
- Before the engine starts, the backdrop is just `bg-bg`, which matches the page at that point. If the background falls back to the static gradient, `SpaceBackground` flags it (`setMirrorFallback`) and the backdrop shows `.space-fallback` sized `100% × 100lvh` from its top-left. That lines up with the page's gradient (max diff 1 to 2 levels, measured by forcing `getContext` to fail).
- `index.css`: I rewrote the header part of the contrast notes and removed the now-unused `--theme-bg-header` / `bg-bg-header` token.
- Problem: the first perf run showed more long frames on Behind the Scenes (see Verification). A trace showed that `drawImage` from the scene canvas pulls that canvas's raster work into the animation frame. Normally it runs a moment later in the commit (`ProduceCanvasResource`), so the work mostly moves rather than grows. The net cost is about +3% main-thread time (about +0.5 ms per frame at 4x). Making the mirror opaque and measuring only on resize didn't change the numbers. Skipping `drawImage` (experiment only) gave about 10 long frames instead of about 17, so most of the cost is the copy itself. I found no cheaper way that stays frame-exact.

**Follow-ups**
- Leader / Chris: the copy costs a little frame time. On Home and Projects it is unmeasurable. On Behind the Scenes, the heaviest scene, which was already at the edge of the frame budget under 4x throttle, long frames go from about 7 to about 17 per 5s (average frame +0.55ms, +3%). This was measured in headless Chromium, where canvas raster runs on the CPU; on a real phone the copy is a GPU blit and should be cheaper, but I could not measure that. If that is not acceptable, the options are: accept a one-frame lag (copy the previous frame before the scene redraws, which may avoid the forced flush; not tried), or keep glass on the bar for that one scene.
- The menu has no line under it any more (per the story). A page element just below it is cut cleanly at the menu's bottom edge (e.g. a button half visible). If that reads oddly, a soft fade at the edge could be a later story.
