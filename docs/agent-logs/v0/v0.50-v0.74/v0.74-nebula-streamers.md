# V0.74 — Nebula streamers look wispy, not straight streaks (2026-10-17)
**Agent:** dev-2
**Branch:** story/v0.74-nebula-streamers · **Status:** done

**What I did**
- **Curving, broken streamers:** the streamers (section 3 of `nebula.ts`) no longer run as long, straight horizontal lines.
  - They follow a smooth "bend" field, so they curve, tilt, fan out and sometimes curl.
  - Their thickness and brightness change along their length: thin threads swell into fainter, wider wisps.
  - They break into pieces of different lengths, and each strand breaks on its own.
- **Still trail downwind and fade:** they still start at bank edges and trail to the right, behind the drift, and fade with distance. The upwind trace now follows the same bend, so the area they cover curves with the strands.
- **Screenshots:** before/after at 375 and 1280 are in `v0.74-nebula-streamers/` next to this log (two JPGs, 141 KB). The hidden-content view is shown so the clouds are easy to see; before is on the left (375) or on top (1280).
- **Per-frame cost unchanged:** the streamers are still baked once into the bank texture. `draw` is not touched, and the texture is the same size (numbers below).
- **Reduced motion and hidden content (V0.61):** still frames with reduced motion, the full-strength hidden view at 375 and 1280, and the sharp 2× bake at 1280 DPR 2 all look right.
- **Widths:** checked at 375×812 and 1280×800.
- Soft-layer banding was not touched; it is left for V0.75.

**How I did it**
- **Why they were straight:** the strand noise had very few lattice cells along x (`px / 2`) and many across, and only a small vertical warp. So each strand was a long horizontal line.
- **Bend field:** a tileable field on the bank's coarse grid (every 4th texel). It is one `offGrid` octave for broad sweeps plus a plain-noise octave for a smaller wiggle.
  - A strand is a line where `y + bend` stays the same. So where the bend changes along x, the strand curves, and neighbouring strands drift apart or closer together.
  - The strength is `STREAMER_BEND = 0.09` of √(w·h): a new knob at the top of the file.
  - The field is periodic on the tile lattice, so the texture still tiles with no seams.
- **Trails follow the bend:** each upwind step looks at row `j + bend(here) − bend(upwind)` instead of the same row. So the streamer area bends with the strands instead of being a horizontal smear of the bank.
- **Thickness and breaks:** two value-noise lookups along the bent strand coordinate.
  - The first sets the cross-section, from about `(1 − off)^14` (thin) to `(1 − off)^5` (wide and fainter).
  - The second cuts the strands into pieces.
  - The along-strand lattice doubled (`px`), so the strands are shorter.
- **Bake cost:** my first version made the streamer pass about 1.6× slower per candidate texel. I timed each part of the pass and fixed it:
  - The cross-section is now `q³` with a cheap early reject, instead of `ridge ** variable`.
  - The bend for each row is interpolated once per row instead of 5 times per texel.
  - The hot loop wraps only out-of-range rows.
  - The whole bake now costs about 3–8% more (table below).
- **Problem: beaded diagonal strands.** Very thin diagonal strands looked beaded at 1× because a texel is about 2.7 CSS px at 1280. Fix: the thinnest strands are a bit wider (exponent about 14, not 22).
