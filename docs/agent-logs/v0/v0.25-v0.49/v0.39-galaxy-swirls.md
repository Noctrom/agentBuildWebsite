**What I did**
- The Contact galaxy now visibly turns. The arm pattern makes one full turn every 90 s (was ~9 min). In 3 s the outer arms move ~12° and the inner stars 30–45°.
- Inner stars orbit faster than the outer arms. Orbit speed is `PATTERN_SPIN * 1.15 / (r + 0.15)`: stars at the edge keep pace with the arms, a star at r = 0.15 turns ~4x faster, and the bulge ~6x (about 15 s per turn).
- The arms still read as a clean spiral after 10 minutes (frames at t = 300 s and 600 s checked). There is no re-seeding and no reset (see How).
- The motion is the same from the first frame on. There is no intro: frame 0 is statistically identical to any later frame.
- Reduce motion: the engine's still frame (time 0) is drawn complete in one call. It looks like the S27 still.
- Position and size are unchanged, except on phones: `cy` moved from 0.76h to 0.75h so the arm tips clear the footer text at 375×812.

**How I did it**
- **Density-wave arms (no smearing):** the glow, knots and dust are baked into one texture (`makeGlow`, the S27 bake minus the star dots). It turns rigidly with the pattern. Stars are drawn live (`src/scenes/galaxyStars.ts`):
  - Each arm star has a fixed radius and an offset from the arm's centre line. That offset drifts at the star's speed relative to the pattern and wraps inside a window of ±width around the arm.
  - Brightness follows a bell `(1-u²)²` over that window and is 0 at its edges. So a star fades out leaving the arm and fades back in entering it, and the wrap is never visible.
  - Offsets start uniform in the window, so the arm shape is the same at every time. Window widths come from the S27 radial jitter converted to an angle along the log spiral.
  - Bulge and disk stars are spread evenly in angle, so differential rotation cannot pile them into rings.
- **Look kept:** same star counts and colors as S27 (1 500 per arm, 700 bulge, 900 disk). Stars are squashed by the tilt as the baked ones were, so size and brightness match. Before/after strips are near identical. The bulge looks slightly cleaner, because the dust no longer cuts the live stars.
- **Drawing cost, iterations:**
  1. One `rect` per star in batched path fills per brightness level. About 8 ms per frame at 4x, too slow.
  2. Bilinear splats into a typed-array buffer, then `putImageData` and one additive `drawImage`. The glow was composited into the same buffer at 30 Hz. Main-thread rAF time was still ~15 ms at 1280 at 4x, because drawing the glow into an offscreen canvas that had just had `putImageData` was slow.
  3. Final:
     - The glow is drawn on the main canvas as in S27.
     - The star buffer is only ever written by `putImageData`.
     - The refill is spread over 3 frames, a third of the stars per frame (stars show new positions 20 times a second; they move ≤0.3 px per frame, ~1 px per update, and the bilinear splat keeps that smooth).
     - Other changes: stamp-based accumulation (only touched pixels are cleared), a sine lookup table, and very faint arm stars skipped.
  - Isolation tests showed that the remaining cost is the splat JS. The buffer `drawImage` and the upload rate made no measurable difference.
- **Text clearance:** the old galaxy was effectively static, but now the whole tilted disk sweeps all orientations. So I checked the full outline: the per-pixel range of galaxy-only light over a 90 s turn, with the static companion smudge excluded.
- **V0.40 prep:** core breathing fits in `drawGlow` (e.g. a core sprite with a time-based alpha), and twinkle as a per-star factor on the light in `splat`. Both are noted in the file header.
