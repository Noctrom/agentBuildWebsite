# V0.60 — Milky Way band across the Contact sky (2026-10-07)
**Agent:** dev-2
**Branch:** story/v0.60-milky-way-band · **Status:** done

**What I did**
- **Desktop:** a diagonal band runs from the lower left to the upper right, as in the photo. It crosses the open sky left of the spiral, runs behind the contact intro, panel and footer text, and passes behind the top of the spiral. Its warm gold middle sits in the open sky just below the contact panel.
- **Phones (375):** the band runs through the galaxy, below the contact text, at a flatter angle. Its warm middle is left of the spiral, where the spiral doesn't hide it.
- **Look:** the centre is gold and amber with a cream highlight. Broken dark brown dust patches and lanes run along the band. The edges go through a greyish mix to blue-white and fade softly into the sky; nothing has a hard edge. The band is full of dense fine stars: blue-white, and gold near the middle. All colours come from V0.59's `MILKY_WAY` palette.
- **Spiral in front:** the band is baked into the Contact sky layer, which is drawn first. The starfield and the whole spiral draw on top of it.
- **Twinkle:** 36 stars twinkle on desktop and 14 on phones. Each has its own period (3–7 s) and a random phase, so they never pulse together. The band itself never moves: no drift and no parallax.
- **AA:** 0 fails at 375/768/1024/1280. The lowest body contrast is 6.17. Numbers below.
- **Reduced motion and Animation switch off:** the scene is fully static.
- **Other pages:** unchanged, pixel-identical to leader.

**How I did it**
- **New file `src/scenes/galaxyBand.ts`:** the band has its own file rather than growing `galaxy.ts` (638 lines) further. It is a new file, so no other agent's file is touched. The brief listed galaxy.ts, galaxyPalette.ts and galaxyStars.ts; I treated a new galaxy-only file as within that scope.
- **Shape (`bandShape`):** the band is defined by a centre line (a point and an angle, 0.55 rad on desktop and 0.26 on phones), a half-width, and where its warm middle sits along it. All values are relative to the galaxy layout, so the band follows the spiral at every size.
- **Bake (`paintBand`):** done once per size.
  - **Low-res pass:** glow, colour and dust go into a low-res bitmap, one cell per 3 CSS px, drawn smoothed. That is what keeps every edge soft.
  - **Per cell:** the band coordinates are computed: along the band, and across it with a gentle noise wander. The band widens at the warm middle.
  - **Glow:** a Gaussian profile across the band, times clumpy fBm (star clouds).
  - **Colour:** a warmth ramp: blue-white, then a greyish mix, then amber, gold and cream.
  - **Dust:** a noise squashed across the band gives patches that run along it, plus thinner ridged lanes inside them. Dust tints toward `MILKY_WAY.dust` and dims the glow, strongest in the middle.
  - **Stars:** fine stars go on at full resolution, placed by rejection sampling on the glow.
  - **Twinkling stars:** picked from bright band cells, off the spiral's disk and outside the text areas.
- **One cached layer:**
  - V0.59's `makeSky` is now a generator. It bakes the sky tint, the band and then the sky's own stars into the same canvas.
  - The per-frame cost is still one `drawImage` for the layer, plus the twinkle sprites (`drawTwinkles`).
  - The layer covers the full width and only the rows the tint and band reach: the whole screen on desktop, about 60% on phones.
- **Building over several frames:** the Contact scene's `create` is now a generator. The band bake yields every 6 rows and every 1,500 stars ("Heavy setup" in `types.ts`). `resize` runs it to the end with `runToEnd`.
  - New state is assigned only after the bake is done, so an abandoned build leaves nothing half-made.
  - The galaxy's own glow, knots, stars and their random sequences are unchanged.
- **Dimming behind text (`textDim`):** the band is dimmed to 75% behind the desktop text, with a soft fade (smoothstep over ~100 px), not a cutout.
  - **Zone:** above ~420–520 px and left of `width/2 + 360`, plus the footer strip. Measured on /contact: the intro reaches 740/851/908/1228 px at widths 768/1024/1280/1920.
  - **Phones:** no dimming, because the band runs below the text there.
  - **Tried first:** with no dimming, the AA scan was still well above 4.5. The lowest values were 6.56 (768), 6.34 (1024) and 6.42 (1280). The dim recovers most of the loss and keeps the band visible.
- **Problems hit:**
  - **Dust lanes:** the first version, a ridged noise across the whole band, made dark worm-like squiggles and navy "holes". Dust as squashed patches aligned with the band axis (not the wandering centre) reads like the photo's rifts.
  - **Phone perf:** the first phone layer was ~520 px tall (reach overestimated). I tightened the reach and flattened the phone angle.
  - Running `git` together with other commands in one shell call is refused in this worktree, so I committed with plain separate calls.
